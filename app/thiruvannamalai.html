🛕 Thiruvannamalai — Arunachaleswarar Temple

About the temple

The temple honours Lord Shiva as Arunachaleswarar (Annamalaiyar) and his consort Parvati as Unnamulai Amman. The Arunachala hill itself is revered as a form of Shiva.

It is one of the largest temple complexes in India, spread over roughly 25 acres with several enclosures (prakarams) and tall gateway towers. The eastern Rajagopuram rises about 66 m in 11 tiers.

The temple is praised in the Tevaram hymns of the Nayanmar saints, and Manikkavasagar's Thiruvempavai is said to have been composed here.

History timeline

    🔥 The column of fire

    Brahma and Vishnu argued over who was supreme. Shiva appeared between them as an endless pillar of fire. Vishnu, as a boar, dug down to find its base; Brahma, as a swan, flew up to find its top. Neither succeeded. Shiva then settled as the Arunachala hill, and the hilltop flame of Karthigai Deepam recalls this moment.

    🌗 Ardhanarishvara

    Parvati performed penance here, after which Shiva granted her the left half of his body — the form known as Ardhanarishvara, celebrated during Karthigai Deepam.

    Suggested route inside the temple

    1. Enter via the eastern Rajagopuram
    2. Thousand-pillared mandapam
    3. Patala Lingam shrine (where Ramana Maharshi meditated)
    4. Sivaganga theertham (temple tank)
    5. Main sanctum — Arunachaleswarar
    6. Unnamulai Amman shrine

    Etiquette & tips

      ♿ Ask at the entrance about wheelchairs and the senior-citizen queue.

      The 14 km walk around Arunachala

      Pilgrims walk around the hill clockwise, usually barefoot, especially on full-moon (Pournami) nights. Eight Lingams — the Ashta Lingams — mark the directions along the path.
